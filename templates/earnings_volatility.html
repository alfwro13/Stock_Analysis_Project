📖 How to Read This Report

Trading around earnings is one of the most volatile events in the stock market. Because earnings reports are binary events (a company either beats expectations or misses them), stocks often gap up or down violently. This scanner acts as an Arbitrage Engine—it compares what the options market thinks will happen against what has historically happened, looking for mathematical mispricings.

1. What are Options?

Options are financial derivative contracts that give you the right, but not the obligation, to buy (a Call option) or sell (a Put option) a stock at a specific price on a specific date. They are essentially bets on future price movement.

Right before an earnings report, options become incredibly expensive. This is because market makers know a massive price jump is coming, so they raise the "premiums" (the cost to buy the contract) to protect themselves. This inflation of price is called Implied Volatility (IV).

2. The Implied Move

The Implied Move tells us exactly how big of a jump the options market is pricing in. Our engine calculates this by looking at the cost of buying an At-The-Money (ATM) Call and an ATM Put expiring right after the earnings date (a strategy known as a Straddle).

Example:
Stock Price = $100
Call Cost = $3.00 | Put Cost = $3.00
Implied Move = 6.00%
In this example, the options market expects the stock to jump 6% up or down. If you buy options, the stock MUST move more than 6% just for you to break even.

3. Historical Avg Move

While the Implied Move looks forward, the Historical Move looks backward to ground us in reality. The engine pulls the exact dates of the company's last 4 quarterly earnings reports (1 year of data).

It mathematically calculates the actual, real-world percentage moves the stock made over a 2-trading-day window surrounding those reports (from the close before the report to the close after), and averages them together. This 2-session window ensures we capture the full price reaction regardless of whether the report was issued Before-Market-Open or After-Market-Close.

4. Edge Score (The Arbitrage)

The Edge Score is our mathematical advantage. It is calculated simply: Historical Move - Implied Move.

Positive Edge (Green)
Implied: 5% | Historical: 10%
Options are UNDERPRICED. The market expects a small move, but historically this stock explodes. Buying options here offers a statistical edge.
Negative Edge (Red)
Implied: 15% | Historical: 3%
Options are OVERPRICED. The market expects chaos, but historically this stock barely moves. This is a setup for an "IV Crush"—selling options here offers an edge.

5. Options Volume

This is the total number of Option contracts that have traded today for the specific strike prices we used in our calculation.

Why it matters: If the volume is extremely low (e.g., under 100), the options are "illiquid." This means the Bid/Ask spread is incredibly wide, making the Implied Move calculation mathematically unreliable. Always look for high volume to ensure the Edge Score is based on trustworthy, highly traded market data.
