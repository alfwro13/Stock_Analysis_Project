What a backtest or an optimized mix does not capture

These tools compare rules on past prices. Before acting on a result, remember what the model leaves out:

  • Hindsight and a short window. Today's tickers are replayed over roughly 1.5–5 years. Companies that failed or were dropped are not in the basket, so results look better than they honestly would have been. The comparison between rules is meaningful; the absolute return is not a forecast.
  • Real trading costs. Costs are a flat percentage per trade. UK stamp duty (0.5% on UK shares), broker minimum fees and wider spreads on small or illiquid holdings are not modelled.
  • Whole shares. The model buys any amount of any ticker. A real account rounds to whole shares (or the broker's fractional limits), so small weights land off target.
  • Currency. Prices are converted to your base currency at each day's FX close. A broker's FX fee on foreign shares is not included.
  • Concentration. Equal weight gives your least-convicted holding as much money as your most-convicted one, and a strategy can leave you holding far more or less of a sector than you intend.
  • Taxes. Tax is not modelled. An ISA has no capital gains tax; other accounts do, and rebalancing can create a taxable gain.
