Key Metrics Explained:
  • PEG Ratio — Peter Lynch's "Fair Value" Test (≤1.0): The PEG ratio answers a simple question: are you paying a fair price for the growth you are getting? Calculated as: Forward P/E ÷ (Earnings Growth % + Dividend Yield %).
    A PEG of 1.0 means the stock's price perfectly reflects its growth rate. A PEG of 0.5 means you are getting £1 of growth for only 50p — a potential bargain. Peter Lynch, who turned $18M into $14 billion, considered PEG ≤1.0 the single most reliable signal of an undervalued growth stock.
  • Revenue Growth (>15% year-on-year): Revenue is the raw proof that customers are buying more of what the company sells. A 15% growth rate means the business is 15% bigger than it was a year ago. This threshold filters out companies whose earnings grow only because of cost-cutting — we want genuine top-line expansion.
  • ROE — Return on Equity (>10%): How efficiently is the business turning your invested capital into profit? This threshold is deliberately looser than the 15% used in Quality Compounders — fast-growing businesses often reinvest heavily, temporarily reducing ROE.
  • Forward P/E — Price to Future Earnings (10–40): Forward P/E uses next year's expected earnings, giving a more forward-looking valuation. The ceiling of 40 is wider than Quality Compounders' 35 because genuine high-growth businesses legitimately command a premium.
  • Market Cap — Company Size (>$500M): A $500M floor keeps us in the range where institutional investors operate, providing better price discovery and exit liquidity.
  • ML Confidence — Machine Learning Score (optional): A second opinion from our quantitative model, trained to predict whether a stock matching GARP criteria actually outperforms the market. Most stocks currently show "—" as ML scoring is still being rolled out across the full universe.
