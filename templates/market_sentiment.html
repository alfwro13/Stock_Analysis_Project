Fear & Greed Index:
Historically, local market bottoms correlate with Extreme Fear (Below 25), indicating capitulation and high-probability entry points. Conversely, Extreme Greed (Above 75) typically signals retail euphoria and institutional distribution, suggesting a defensive posture or profit-taking is warranted.
US Cost of Capital:
The 3-Day Velocity Gauge tracks the short-term rate of change in yields. Rapid spikes (Red) cause severe equity compression. The Yield vs S&P 500 chart visualizes the long-term inverse correlation between borrowing costs and market health.
{{ vix_spy_html | safe }}
How to read this chart:
This visualization overlays the S&P 500 against the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX). The VIX measures the stock market's expectation of volatility based on S&P 500 index options. A VIX level Above 20 indicates high turbulence and institutional hedging, marking a Volatile regime. A VIX level Above 30 signals a Crash regime, characterized by extreme fear and erratic price swings.

UK Dynamics Explained:
UK Cost of Capital: Just like the US, rising borrowing costs (10Y Gilt Yields) typically compress equity valuations.
Currency Impact: Because roughly 70-75% of FTSE 100 corporate revenue is generated outside the UK (priced in USD and EUR), a weakening Pound Sterling (GBP) often drives the FTSE 100 up. This is because their overseas earnings are artificially inflated when converted back into GBP. Look for an inverse correlation between the GBP/USD rate and the FTSE 100 points.
