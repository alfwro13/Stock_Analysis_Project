🤖 Machine Learning & Risk Metrics
ML Confidence Score

The mathematical probability (0-100%) output by a Soft-Voting Ensemble (XGBoost + Random Forest) that the stock will generate a >3% return over the next 10 trading days (entry at T+1 close, exit at T+10 close).

Value at Risk — 1-day, 95% (Historical Simulation)

An institutional metric for downside tail-risk that defines the worst-case scenario under normal market conditions.

  • The Safe Zone: There is a 95% probability that the asset will not lose more than the VaR percentage in a single trading day.
  • The Danger Zone: There is a 5% probability (roughly 1 out of every 20 days) that the asset will drop by the VaR amount or worse.
EXAMPLE: On a $100,000 position with a 12.76% VaR, you are 95% confident your daily loss will not exceed $12,760.
Conditional VaR (CVaR)

Also known as "Expected Shortfall". While VaR tells you where the cliff edge is, CVaR tells you how far the drop is once you fall off.

It calculates the average expected loss on the specific days that the VaR threshold is actually breached (the 5% tail events). This measures the true severity of market shocks, flash crashes, or extreme sell-offs beyond the VaR boundary.

VADER Sentiment

Natural Language Processing (NLP) engine that reads the latest news headlines and scores the narrative from -1.0 to +1.0.

Turbulence Index & Regimes

A macro filter using EWMA realized volatility of SPY and FTSE. VIX is shown alongside for reference but is not part of the regime signal. Classifies market into Normal, Volatile, or Crash regimes.

Market Regime — Price HMM

A 3-state Hidden Markov Model trained on 5 years of SPY daily log-returns and 20-day EWMA annualised volatility (λ=0.94). States are sorted by mean volatility so the ordering is stable across daily retrains: State 0 = Bull (low vol, positive returns), State 1 = Chop (elevated vol, indecisive), State 2 = Crash (high vol, negative returns). The current state and confidence probability are shown on the Trap Monitor page; the full Viterbi history, transition matrix, and per-regime statistics are at /market-regime. When the regime transitions, a Nextcloud alert fires.

Distinct from the Macro HMM in macro_ai_engine.py (which is trained on M2, jobless claims, HY spread, and yield curve to predict macro calendar event impacts). This one operates on price and volatility only — it is a market-microstructure signal, not a macro-fundamental one.
🌍 Macro Regime Label (Yield Curve Allocator)

A single named state synthesised from yield curve shape, CPI inflation, high-yield credit spreads, the 10-year TIPS real yield, and the HMM hidden state. Five labels are defined: Risk-On (positive curve, low inflation, tight spreads), Late Cycle (flattening curve, rising CPI), Stagflation (high CPI with financial stress or negative real yield), Contraction (inverted curve plus recession-mode HMM), and Recovery (transitioning out of contraction with improving HMM).

Computed by regime_engine.classify_macro_regime() each time the systemic threat calculator runs. Stored as regime_label in macro_regimes. Powers the Macro Regime Allocator tool at /macro-allocator.
Portfolio Regime Alignment Score

A 0–100 score measuring how closely your current asset class weights (equities, bonds, commodities, cash) match the historically optimal weights for the detected macro regime. Computed using cosine similarity between the current allocation vector and the ideal allocation midpoint vector. 100 = perfect match; below 50 = meaningfully misaligned. The score is informational — it does not account for individual conviction, tax, or liquidity constraints.

Isolation Forest Anomaly Score

An unsupervised machine learning signal that detects when a stock is behaving in a statistically unusual way across six simultaneous dimensions: intraday volume relative to its 20-day average, RSI momentum, daily return percentage, distance from the 50-day SMA, 20-day historical volatility, and beta-adjusted market sensitivity. Unlike a single-threshold rule (e.g. "volume > 1.5×"), the Isolation Forest notices compound anomalies — for example, a volume spike that would be unremarkable on its own, but is occurring alongside an RSI divergence and an SMA break simultaneously.

The model is trained separately for each ticker on approximately one year of daily data, so it learns what normal looks like specifically for that stock, not the broad market. The output is a score between 0.00 and 1.00:

  • 0.00 – 0.40 (Normal): The stock is trading within its typical multi-dimensional behaviour pattern. No action required.
  • 0.40 – 0.70 (Elevated): Some unusual characteristics present but not outside normal market noise. Worth monitoring.
  • 0.70 – 1.00 (Alert Zone): The combination of signals is statistically rare for this specific ticker. The system fires a pre-alert to Nextcloud. This does not predict direction — it flags that something structural is changing before a price threshold is breached.
KEY DISTINCTION: This is an early-warning layer, not a crash detector. A score above 0.70 while the price is flat or rising often indicates silent institutional repositioning — accumulation or distribution at scale — before the price reaction is visible.

Models are retrained nightly at 18:30 (Mon–Fri) after market close and are stored as per-ticker .joblib files. Historical scores are backfilled across the full quant_signals history so the 90-day chart on each stock detail page is populated immediately after training.

Market Stress Score

A market-wide Isolation Forest trained daily on 2 years of six macro features: VIX level, VIX relative to its 20-day average (spike detector), HYG credit ETF daily return, US 10-year yield daily change, SPY volume z-score, and SPY daily return. Unlike the per-ticker anomaly score — which asks "is this stock unusual?" — the Market Stress Score asks "is the market structure itself unusual?" A score near 1.0 means the joint distribution of these six signals is in territory that only occurred in roughly 5% of all trading days over the past two years.

The output is a score between 0.00 and 1.00:

  • 0.00 – 0.50 (Normal): The macro environment is operating within its typical multivariate range.
  • 0.50 – 0.75 (Elevated): One or more macro signals are stretched; monitor for follow-through.
  • 0.75 – 1.00 (Stress Alert): The combination of signals is statistically rare. A Nextcloud alert fires if the score remains above 0.75 for two consecutive trading days.
COMPLEMENTARY TO HMM: The HMM classifies which regime the market is in (Bull / Chop / Crash). The Market Stress Score measures how anomalous the current regime environment is relative to its own history. A Crash-regime day with a low stress score is a routine bad day; a Chop-regime day with a high stress score may precede a regime transition.

📅 Earnings Volatility & Options Mispricing
Implied Move (ATM Straddle)

Percentage move the options market expects based on the cost of ATM Call + Put.

Historical Average Move

The average absolute percentage price gap observed over the last 4 quarterly earnings events.

Mathematical Edge Score

The variance between Historical Move and Implied Move. Positive means options are underpriced.

🎯 The Multi-Regime Strategy (Scoring & Tags)
⚡ MACD Reversal

Used for bottom-fishing when selling momentum has mathematically exhausted itself.

TRIGGER: MACD line crosses above the Signal line.
🔥 VCP Breakout (Volatility Contraction)

Coiled spring setup: institutions absorbing supply without causing price spikes.

TRIGGER: Weekly variance < 2.5% AND volume dry-up > 20%.
🎭 Bull Trap / Dead Cat Bounce

A false recovery after a sell-off where prices bounce on suspiciously low volume while still trading below the 20-day EMA. Retail buyers purchase the dip, but institutional conviction is absent — the bounce is driven by short-covering, not genuine demand. The second leg down typically follows.

SIGNAL: Close < 20-EMA AND recent up-days AND avg volume on up-days / avg volume on down-days < 0.75–0.90.
🪤 Bear Trap / False Breakdown

A false support breakdown: price briefly pierces the lower Bollinger Band or a key swing low intraday, enticing short sellers to open positions, but then reverses and closes back above support on low volume. The short sellers are “trapped” as the price rallies against them.

SIGNAL: Intraday low < Bollinger Lower Band AND close recovers above support AND breakdown volume < 1.2× 20-day average.
💥 Capitulation (Final Flush)

A violent, high-volume selling climax where leveraged funds and retail traders are forced to liquidate at any price. The statistical signature is volume more than 3 standard deviations above the 20-day mean, combined with an extremely oversold RSI. A candle closing in the upper half of its range (long lower wick) suggests institutions absorbed the panic — this is the potential true bottom.

SIGNAL: Volume > μ20 + 3σ AND RSI < 30 AND close in upper 50% of day’s range.
🏗️ Wyckoff Accumulation Phase

Named after Richard Wyckoff, this is the base-building period after capitulation where institutional buyers quietly absorb supply without moving the price. The quantitative fingerprint is a Bollinger Band squeeze (band width < 2% of price, at a 20-day low) combined with declining ATR and drying volume. A high-volume breakout above the upper band confirms the accumulation phase is complete.

SIGNAL: BB width < 2% AND at 20-day low AND ATR now < 70% of 20-day average AND 5-day avg vol < 70% of 20-day average.

🕯️ Candlestick Anatomy Masterclass
🔨 The Hammer (Bullish Rejection)
Sellers drove price down, but institutional buyers rejected the lows violently.
🐂 Bullish Engulfing
Current day's green body completely overwhelms the previous red body.
🌅 Morning Star (3-Candle)

Crown jewel of bottom-fishing. Panic (D1), Indecision (D2), then violent Reversal (D3).

🪖 Three White Soldiers (3-Candle)
Three consecutive bullish candles, each opening inside the prior body and closing higher. Sustained institutional buying — not a one-day spike.
🌱 Bullish Harami Cross / 🕸️ Bearish Harami Cross (2-Candle)

A Doji that forms entirely inside the body of the prior large candle. More significant than a standalone Doji because the containment shows the dominant trend has stalled: on a Bullish Harami Cross, sellers have exhausted themselves inside a large red candle; on a Bearish Harami Cross, buyers have run out of momentum inside a large green one.

🗡️ Piercing Line (2-Candle)
Bullish D2 opens below D1's close but fights back above D1's midpoint. Weaker than Engulfing, but buyers are clearly defending the low.
📈 Technical Analysis (Chart Momentum)
Moving Averages (5D, 10D, 21D, 50D, 200D)

Smoothing price fluctuations to reveal trend. 50D is medium health; 200D is the institutional line.

RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Measures velocity of price changes. Overbought > 70; Oversold < 30.

ATR Stop-Loss (Average True Range)

Volatility-adjusted trailing stop dynamically pinned between 1.8× and 3.0× ATRs below the current price.

The multiplier automatically adapts to the stock's historical volatility contraction/expansion (ATR Stability Ratio) and widens defensively during high-risk macro events to prevent noise whipsaws.

This line isn't just an arbitrary number; it is the mathematical boundary of the stock's standard volatility envelope.

As long as the stock stays above the ATR stop, its uptrend is structurally intact. Any drop is just a "normal pullback" (a true dip).

If the stock drops below the ATR stop, it has mathematically violated its volatility envelope. It is no longer a normal pullback; the structural trend has broken, and the asset is in freefall.

🎯 Dip Radar — Intraday Capitulation Detection
Intraday Mean Reversion

The quantitative principle underlying Dip Radar. Intraday prices frequently overshoot to the downside during sell-offs as panic, margin calls, and stop-loss cascades force sellers to hit bids indiscriminately. Once selling pressure is mathematically exhausted — a state called capitulation — price tends to snap back toward its fair intraday value (VWAP). Dip Radar measures four independent dimensions of this exhaustion and combines them into a single score.

Reversal Score (0–100)

The composite signal output by the Dip Radar engine. Points are awarded for each confirmed exhaustion condition:

+30 pts: RSI < 25 (extreme oversold) — or +15 pts: RSI < 30 (oversold)
+25 pts: Price below the Lower Bollinger Band at 2.5σ deviation
+20 pts: Price more than 2.5σ below the intraday VWAP
+25 pts: Volume Capitulation — current down-candle's volume exceeds the 20-candle SMA by more than 3 standard deviations
Alert threshold: Score ≥ 65. Below this, selling pressure is still present. Above this, the system has detected a high-probability exhaustion zone.

The maximum score is 100 (all four conditions simultaneously active). A score of 65–80 indicates a likely short-term bottom. A score of 80–100 indicates a potential capitulation washout — the highest-probability reversal setup.

VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)

The intraday "true fair value" of a stock, calculated by weighting each minute's price by the trading volume at that time. Unlike a simple moving average, VWAP gives more weight to the prices where the most money changed hands — it is the benchmark institutional traders use to evaluate execution quality.

Dip Radar uses the intraday VWAP anchored from the market open (09:30 ET) and calculates a rolling 30-bar standard deviation band around it. A price trading 2.5σ below VWAP means it has deviated so far from fair value that a statistical mean-reversion is likely.

VWAP = Σ(Typical Price × Volume) / Σ(Volume), reset at each market open.
Bollinger Bands (2.5σ Extreme Variant)

Standard Bollinger Bands use 2 standard deviations (σ). Dip Radar deliberately uses a wider 2.5σ band over a 20-candle window to filter out routine volatility and identify only statistically extreme price dislocations. A price piercing the 2.5σ lower band on 1-minute data occurs in less than 1% of candles under normal distribution — when it does, it signals that momentum has overextended to the downside.

BB Lower = 20-period SMA − (2.5 × 20-period Std Dev)
Volume Capitulation (Climax Volume)

The most powerful single signal in the Dip Radar algorithm. Capitulation occurs when panicking sellers all exit at the same time, producing a down-candle with a volume spike that is 3 or more standard deviations above the 20-candle rolling average. This is the quantitative fingerprint of "weak hands washing out" — forced sellers (margin calls, stop-loss orders, retail panic) exhausting their supply of stock.

After capitulation, there are no more forced sellers left in the short term, removing the primary source of downward pressure. The remaining participants are either long-term holders or new buyers looking for value — setting up a sharp reversal.

Condition: Volume > (20-candle Volume SMA) + (3 × 20-candle Volume Std Dev) — AND — current candle closes lower than the prior candle.
Session-Scoped Monitoring

Dip Radar is deliberately not a persistent background monitor. It is activated on-demand per ticker from the stock detail page and automatically deactivates at 16:05 ET (21:05 BST in summer / 22:05 GMT in winter) each trading day. This design prevents alert fatigue and avoids scanning noise outside of active market hours. The deduplication system (alert_state table) ensures that only the first breach of the score threshold fires an alert — repeat scans at the same high score are silently suppressed until a new session arms the ticker again.

📊 ETF Price Predictor
ETF Price Predictor

A configurable tool that estimates an ETF's next-session opening price by weighting the after-hours returns of its top constituent holdings. Users configure the ETF ticker and up to 20 constituent tickers with weights in Settings. The system normalises weights to 100% and runs two engines: a holdings-weighted basket return engine (primary) and an OLS regression engine (fallback).

Holdings Engine

The primary prediction engine. For each constituent, the engine measures the price change since the ETF's home-exchange close (post-close intraday signal) or since the prior daily close (daily signal). It then weights these returns by each constituent's portfolio weight to compute a weighted basket return, applies an FX adjustment if the ETF and its constituents are denominated in different currencies, and projects the ETF's next open from its last close.

Predicted Price = Last Close × (1 + Weighted Return + FX Adjustment)
Regression Engine

The fallback prediction engine. Uses 60+ days of history to fit an OLS regression model: ETF next-morning return = α + β × average constituent daily return. When fewer than 3 constituents have usable data (holdings engine returns None), the regression engine is used instead. Reports R², beta, and a 95% confidence interval derived from regression residuals.

Constituent Weights

The percentage allocation each ticker represents within the configured ETF prediction basket. Weights are automatically normalised to sum to 100% regardless of input values. Weights at the time of each prediction are saved as a snapshot so editing the config later does not distort historical accuracy records.

📉 Historical Stress Tester
Beta-Adjusted Scenario Shock

The core formula used by the stress tester to estimate each holding's loss during a historical crash. Beta measures how much a stock amplifies (or dampens) the market's move: a stock with β=1.5 is expected to fall 50% more than the market on a down day.

Holding Drop (%) = Market Crash % × Beta × Sector Multiplier

The result is then multiplied by the holding's current value to produce a concrete monetary loss estimate in your base currency. Drops are capped at −95% to reflect that equity rarely goes to zero in a systemic crash.

Sector Multiplier

A per-sector adjustment that reflects how much more or less a given sector fell compared to the broad market during a specific historical crisis. A multiplier above 1.0 means the sector was hit harder than the index; below 1.0 means it held up better. A negative multiplier means the sector actually gained during the crash (e.g. Energy in the 2022 inflation shock).

Examples from the Dot-com crash: Technology ×2.2 (NASDAQ fell 78% vs S&P's 49%), Consumer Defensive ×0.4 (fell far less than the market), Energy ×0.5.

Why Not Historical Replay?

The stress tester uses a parametric model rather than replaying actual historical prices for two reasons: (1) local price data only covers the past 2 years — the GFC, Dot-com crash, COVID crash, and 2022 shock are all outside this window; (2) many holdings (newer ETFs, recently-listed stocks) simply did not exist during those crashes. The beta-shock approach works for any holding regardless of listing date, and is transparent about its assumptions.

🧠 System Methodology (The "Why")
Quantamental "Dual-Lens" Architecture

Fundamentals decide WHAT to buy; Quantitative technicals decide WHEN to buy.

Hierarchical Signal Purity

Strict evaluation priority: 3-candle patterns override 2-candle patterns to prevent contradictions.

FinBERT NLP Sentiment Engine

An institutional-grade Natural Language Processing model powered by HuggingFace's ProsusAI/finbert. It continuously ingests the latest financial news, truncates the text to fit strict BERT token limits, and processes Wall Street narratives to output a standardized sentiment score.

The raw float scores are mapped into five distinct emotional states: Euphoria, Bullish, Neutral, Bearish, and Extreme Fear.

Architectural Note (The Yield Trap): Sovereign bond yields (e.g., US 30Y or UK Gilt) are strictly excluded from NLP scoring. NLP models natively interpret words like "surging" or "breaking out" as positive. However, surging risk-free yields are fundamentally toxic for equities. Excluding them prevents critical inverse-logic UI errors.
