🎯 The Multi-Regime Strategy (Scoring & Tags)

Our system operates on a "Dual-Lens" architecture. The 0-100 Score tells you if an asset is healthy to hold. The Setup Tags tell you exactly when it is mathematically optimal to buy or sell.

⚡ MACD Reversal

An early trend reversal setup. The MACD algorithm calculates when selling momentum has mathematically exhausted itself.

TRIGGER: Fast MACD crosses above the Slow Signal line while below the zero axis.

Strategy: Used for bottom-fishing. Buy the asset as the bleeding stops, before the moving averages even realize the trend has changed.

🔥 VCP Breakout (Volatility Contraction)

Pioneered by Mark Minervini. It identifies stocks that are winding up like a coiled spring. Institutional investors are quietly absorbing supply without causing the price to spike.

TRIGGER: Weekly price variance < 2.5% over 3 weeks AND average volume dries up by > 20%.

Strategy: The safest, highest-probability setup. Buy when the tag appears, expecting an explosive breakout as soon as volume returns.

👑 Market Leader

Capital flows to where it is treated best. This setup scans the Relative Strength (RS) of the stock against the S&P 500.

TRIGGER: 60-day RS linear slope is sharply positive AND RS is near a 60-day high.

Strategy: Add monthly capital to these stocks. Do not put money into laggards when market leaders are absorbing all the liquidity.

🚨 Divergence Warning (Circuit Breaker)

The ultimate risk-management alarm. It detects when the price is lying to retail investors.

TRIGGER: Price makes a new High, but the RSI momentum oscillator makes a Lower High.

Strategy: Sell or trim immediately. This indicates institutional distribution (selling) into retail buying pressure. A dump is imminent.

🕯️ Candlestick Anatomy Masterclass

Unlike line charts, candlesticks reveal the psychological war between buyers and sellers within a single timeframe. The Body represents the Open and Close. The Wicks represent the extreme Highs and Lows reached intraday before the price retreated.

🔨 The Hammer (Bullish Rejection)
The Psychology: Sellers aggressively took control early in the day, driving the price down and creating a massive lower wick. However, institutional buyers stepped in, absorbed all the selling pressure, and violently pushed the price back up to close near the highs.

System Rule: Lower wick is at least 2x the size of the body. Upper wick is practically non-existent.
🌠 The Shooting Star (Bearish Rejection)
The Psychology: The exact opposite of a hammer. Euphoric retail buyers pushed the price to new highs intraday. However, institutions used this liquidity to secretly dump their shares, crushing the price back down to the lows before the close.

System Rule: Upper wick is at least 2x the size of the body. Indicates an imminent trend reversal to the downside.
🐂 Bullish Engulfing
The Psychology: A two-day pattern. Day 1 is a standard red day. On Day 2, the stock opens lower but buyers step in with overwhelming force, driving the price so high that the green body completely "engulfs" the previous day's red body. It signifies a massive shift in momentum from bears to bulls.
⚖️ The Doji (Total Indecision)
The Psychology: A brutal tug-of-war occurred all day, but neither side won. The opening price and closing price are mathematically almost identical. A Doji acts as a "pause" in the market. It often precedes a violent breakout, as the market is winding up to pick a decisive direction.
🌅 The Morning Star (3-Candle Reversal)
The Psychology: The crown jewel of bottom-fishing setups.
Day 1: Panic. A massive red candle as weak hands sell.
Day 2: Exhaustion. The stock gaps down, but selling dries up, forming a Doji (Indecision).
Day 3: The Reversal. Institutional buyers step in heavily, printing a massive green candle that pushes more than 50% into Day 1's red body, trapping short-sellers.
📈 Technical Analysis (Chart Momentum)
Moving Averages (5D, 10D, 21D, 50D, 200D)

A moving average smooths out daily price fluctuations to reveal the underlying trend.

  • 5D & 10D: Hyper-short-term momentum. If price is above, it is running hot.
  • 21D (The Swing Line): The primary short-term trend line.
  • 50D (The Health Line): Medium-term health. A stock is "healthy" if above this.
  • 200D (The Institutional Line): The ultimate dividing line. Large funds rarely buy below this.
IF 5D > 10D > 21D THEN: Short-term trends are perfectly aligned.
MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)

A momentum oscillator plotted on the 4th row of the macro chart. It shows the relationship between two moving averages of a stock's price.

  • MACD Line (Blue): The fast momentum line.
  • Signal Line (Orange): The slow trailing line.
  • Histogram (Bars): The difference between the MACD and the Signal. Green bars mean momentum is accelerating upward.
RSI (Relative Strength Index)

A momentum oscillator plotted on the 3rd row of the chart that measures the speed of recent price changes.

IF RSI > 70 THEN: Overbought. It has risen too fast and is likely to pull back.
IF RSI < 30 THEN: Oversold. It has dropped too fast and may bounce.
Bollinger Bands

The dashed gray lines encasing the price on the main macro chart. They measure standard deviations of volatility away from a simple moving average.

  • When price hits the Upper Band, the asset is statistically over-extended and may face resistance.
  • When price hits the Lower Band, the asset is statistically crushed and may find support.
  • When the bands squeeze tightly together, a massive explosive move is imminent.
On-Balance Volume (OBV)

Plotted on the bottom row of the macro chart. OBV adds the day's volume if the stock closed up, and subtracts it if the stock closed down. It tells us if "smart money" is quietly accumulating or distributing shares.

IF OBV is trending UP while Price is flat THEN: Institutions are accumulating heavily.
ATR Stop-Loss (Average True Range)

Calculates the average daily price swing. The system's trailing Stop Loss is pinned 2x ATRs below the current price.

IF Price drops below ATR Stop-Loss THEN: Normal volatility patterns are broken. Sell to manage risk.
🏢 Fundamental Valuation (Company Health)
Valuation Metrics (P/E & P/B)

Standard measures of a company's price tag relative to its real-world performance.

  • Trailing P/E: Current price divided by actual earnings over the last 12 months.
  • Forward P/E: Current price divided by estimated earnings for the next 12 months.
  • Price-to-Book (P/B): Price divided by the literal net asset value of the company (if it were liquidated today).
Peter Lynch Fair Value PEG

A custom adaptation of the PEG ratio that compares the standard P/E ratio to the company's actual earnings growth rate. Standard PEG often uses unreliable forward estimates.

IF Peter Lynch PEG < 1.0 THEN: Severely undervalued relative to its growth.
IF Peter Lynch PEG > 2.0 THEN: Highly overvalued. Growth does not justify the price.
Financial Safety (Debt & Liquidity)
  • Debt-to-Equity: > 2.0 can be a massive bankruptcy risk in high-interest-rate environments.
  • Current Ratio: Measures if they can pay their short-term bills. < 1.0 means they have more immediate debts than cash on hand.
Institutional Sentiment
  • Short Interest: The percentage of available shares that hedge funds are actively betting against. >10% implies extreme pessimism and volatility.
  • Beta (Risk): Volatility relative to the S&P 500. Beta > 1 is more volatile (High Risk), Beta < 1 is safer.
🧠 System Methodology (The "Why")

This section documents the underlying philosophy and architectural decisions behind the system's quantitative engine.

The "Dual-Lens" Architecture (Quantamental)

Pure fundamental investors often fall into "Value Traps"—buying cheap companies that continue to bleed money for years. Pure technical traders often get wiped out buying junk companies simply because a chart looked pretty right before an earnings crash. Quantamental investing bridges this gap. The system uses Fundamentals to decide WHAT is safe to buy, and Quantitative Technicals to mathematically dictate exactly WHEN to buy it.

Hierarchical Candlestick Evaluation

In older versions of our engine, a candle could simultaneously trigger multiple overlapping patterns (e.g., flagging as both a neutral Doji and a bearish Shooting Star), causing visual contradictions. The current engine uses a strict Hierarchical Priority System. Because larger timeframes carry more psychological weight, the system evaluates 3-candle patterns first (Tier 1), then 2-candle patterns (Tier 2), and finally 1-candle patterns (Tier 3). If a Tier 1 pattern fires, evaluation immediately halts, ensuring absolute signal purity.

Pseudo-Daily Intraday Math

Candlestick patterns are designed for Daily charts; applying their rigid rules directly to 5-minute charts generates extreme noise. To provide live, intraday pattern alerts without the noise, the system intercepts the raw 5-minute ticks, mathematically compresses them into a single "pseudo-daily" candle, and evaluates that live formation against the previous closed days. This tells you what the Daily momentum is shaping up to be before the market officially closes.

Ghostfolio Integration & VWAP Logic

The system serves as a powerful overlay for Ghostfolio. Instead of merely copying Ghostfolio's data, the engine extracts the raw transactional history across all isolated accounts (Micro Ledgers) and aggregates them into a global Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP). This prevents portfolio fragmentation and ensures foreign assets (e.g., USD stocks held by a UK investor) are dynamically converted to a unified base currency using live Exchange Rates, providing an institutional-grade assessment of true Unrealized P&L.
