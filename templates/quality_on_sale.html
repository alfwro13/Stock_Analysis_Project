How to use: Finds companies that have spent months falling — yet remain profitable, low-debt, and high-return on equity.
When to use: Use this to systematically deploy idle cash during corrections.
The Benefit: Long-term investors make their money buying quality businesses when the market is fearful. This report surfaces those moments automatically.

Key Metrics Explained:
  • % Above 52-Week Low: A value of 2% means the stock is only 2% above its annual floor. The lower this number, the more "on sale" the stock currently is. We cap at 15% to keep only the most beaten-down candidates.
  • ROE — Return on Equity (>10%): A falling stock price does not always mean a failing business. We require >10% to confirm the business has not deteriorated. This is the core "quality" gate. A company with ROE <10% is likely falling for a fundamental reason, not a temporary one.
  • Debt/Equity — Debt to Equity Ratio (<1.5x): When a stock is falling, you want confidence the company can survive the downturn. High-debt companies near 52-week lows are especially dangerous — a revenue shortfall can trigger a debt spiral.
  • Profit Margin (>5%): A margin above 5% confirms the business is generating real profit. Thin or negative margins near a 52-week low are a classic "falling knife" profile.
  • Trailing P/E (<25): A P/E below 25 ensures we are buying at a price that reflects the earnings reality, not lingering optimism from a prior bull run.
  • Quant Score (≥50): Acts as a final cross-check: a stock can pass all four fundamental gates above yet still fail the quant score if there is hidden weakness in its technicals.
