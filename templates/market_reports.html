💎 Quality Compounders↑ top

The "Buy and Hold" Screener. High capital efficiency, strong margins, and low debt trading at reasonable valuations.

How to use: These are structurally resilient businesses with proven pricing power and low leverage.
When to use: Use this to build the core foundational pillars of your long-term portfolio.
The Benefit: Filters out both value traps (dying companies) and euphoria traps (overpriced momentum), surfacing only high-quality compounders with a Quant Score ≥60.

Ticker Company Name Sector Close Price ROE Margin Debt/Eq P/E Quant Score
Key Metrics Explained:
  • ROE — Return on Equity (>15%): Think of ROE as a measure of how good the company is at turning shareholder money into profit. If you gave a business £100 to invest and it returned £20 profit, that is an ROE of 20% — excellent. A business returning only £5 on every £100 (5% ROE) is inefficient with your capital. We require >15% to ensure management is genuinely skilled at allocating resources, not just surviving on past glories.
  • Margin — Profit Margin (>10%): For every £1 of revenue the company earns, how many pence does it actually keep as profit after paying all its costs? A 10% margin means it keeps 10p. A 2% margin means it keeps 2p — one bad quarter and that margin disappears entirely. High margins signal a genuine competitive advantage (a "moat"): the company can charge premium prices because customers have few alternatives. Businesses with thin margins are fragile — any cost spike (wages, energy, raw materials) can wipe out profitability overnight.
  • Debt/Equity — Debt to Equity Ratio (<1.0): This tells you how much of the business is funded by borrowed money versus the owners' own money. A ratio of 0.5 means for every £1 of equity, the company has borrowed 50p — conservative and safe. A ratio of 3.0 means the company has borrowed £3 for every £1 it actually owns — dangerously exposed if interest rates rise or revenues dip. We cap at <1.0 because heavily indebted companies can look profitable in good times but collapse when conditions get tough.
  • P/E — Price-to-Earnings Ratio (10–35): The P/E ratio tells you how many years of current earnings you are paying for upfront. A P/E of 20 means you are paying £20 today for every £1 of annual profit the company generates.
    Why does the floor matter (P/E < 10)? Extremely cheap stocks are often cheap for a very good reason — the market expects earnings to collapse. These are called "value traps."
    Why does the ceiling matter (P/E > 35)? Very expensive stocks are priced for perfection. Any earnings miss, any slowdown, and the share price falls sharply.
    The 10–35 window targets the sweet spot: solid businesses at fair, sustainable valuations.
  • Quant Score (≥60): Our composite score blends all the above metrics — plus momentum and mean-reversion signals — into a single 0–100 number. Think of it as an overall health grade. A score of 60 is a solid B grade: not perfect, but fundamentally sound across every dimension we measure. Stocks below 60 may pass one or two individual screens but show weakness somewhere else in the picture.

🚀 GARP — Peter Lynch Tenbaggers↑ top

Growth At a Reasonable Price. Cheap-relative-to-growth equities from the FTSE100 + S&P500 universe that pass Peter Lynch's yield-adjusted PEG threshold.

How to use: Stocks here combine genuine growth (revenue +15%, profitable, growing earnings) with a valuation cheap enough to compound multi-bagger upside.
When to use: Ideal for monthly trading-account top-ups when you want growth exposure without paying euphoria multiples.
The Benefit: Lynch's PEG ≤1.0 ensures you are not overpaying for the growth. The $500M market-cap floor excludes micro-cap pump-and-dumps.

Ticker Company Name Sector Close Price PEG (Lynch) Rev Growth ROE Fwd P/E Market Cap ML
Key Metrics Explained:
  • PEG Ratio — Peter Lynch's "Fair Value" Test (≤1.0): The PEG ratio answers a simple question: are you paying a fair price for the growth you are getting? Calculated as: Forward P/E ÷ (Earnings Growth % + Dividend Yield %).
    A PEG of 1.0 means the stock's price perfectly reflects its growth rate. A PEG of 0.5 means you are getting £1 of growth for only 50p — a potential bargain. Peter Lynch, who turned $18M into $14 billion, considered PEG ≤1.0 the single most reliable signal of an undervalued growth stock.
  • Revenue Growth (>15% year-on-year): Revenue is the raw proof that customers are buying more of what the company sells. A 15% growth rate means the business is 15% bigger than it was a year ago. This threshold filters out companies whose earnings grow only because of cost-cutting — we want genuine top-line expansion.
  • ROE — Return on Equity (>10%): How efficiently is the business turning your invested capital into profit? This threshold is deliberately looser than the 15% used in Quality Compounders — fast-growing businesses often reinvest heavily, temporarily reducing ROE.
  • Forward P/E — Price to Future Earnings (10–40): Forward P/E uses next year's expected earnings, giving a more forward-looking valuation. The ceiling of 40 is wider than Quality Compounders' 35 because genuine high-growth businesses legitimately command a premium.
  • Market Cap — Company Size (>$500M): A $500M floor keeps us in the range where institutional investors operate, providing better price discovery and exit liquidity.
  • ML Confidence — Machine Learning Score (optional): A second opinion from our quantitative model, trained to predict whether a stock matching GARP criteria actually outperforms the market. Most stocks currently show "—" as ML scoring is still being rolled out across the full universe.

💎 Quality on Sale — 52-Week Low Bargains↑ top

The "Structural Value" Screener. High-quality businesses the market has thrown out with the bathwater — price near a 52-week low, but fundamentals still intact.

How to use: Finds companies that have spent months falling — yet remain profitable, low-debt, and high-return on equity.
When to use: Use this to systematically deploy idle cash during corrections.
The Benefit: Long-term investors make their money buying quality businesses when the market is fearful. This report surfaces those moments automatically.

Ticker Company Name Sector Close Price % Above 52w Low ROE Debt/Eq Margin P/E Quant Score
Key Metrics Explained:
  • % Above 52-Week Low: A value of 2% means the stock is only 2% above its annual floor. The lower this number, the more "on sale" the stock currently is. We cap at 15% to keep only the most beaten-down candidates.
  • ROE — Return on Equity (>10%): A falling stock price does not always mean a failing business. We require >10% to confirm the business has not deteriorated. This is the core "quality" gate. A company with ROE <10% is likely falling for a fundamental reason, not a temporary one.
  • Debt/Equity — Debt to Equity Ratio (<1.5x): When a stock is falling, you want confidence the company can survive the downturn. High-debt companies near 52-week lows are especially dangerous — a revenue shortfall can trigger a debt spiral.
  • Profit Margin (>5%): A margin above 5% confirms the business is generating real profit. Thin or negative margins near a 52-week low are a classic "falling knife" profile.
  • Trailing P/E (<25): A P/E below 25 ensures we are buying at a price that reflects the earnings reality, not lingering optimism from a prior bull run.
  • Quant Score (≥50): Acts as a final cross-check: a stock can pass all four fundamental gates above yet still fail the quant score if there is hidden weakness in its technicals.

Mean Reversion Screener↑ top

Find stocks fundamentally in a long-term uptrend (>200D SMA) experiencing a severe short-term panic sell-off.

How to use: This is a "bargain hunter" tool. It finds stocks that are strong long-term but have suddenly "snapped" like a rubber band due to short-term panic.
When to use: Use this when you want to buy a high-quality stock "on sale" during a temporary dip.
The Benefit: It prevents you from "chasing" high prices — instead it identifies a low-risk entry point where the price is likely to "snap back."

Ticker Company Name Sector Country Close Price RSI (14) 200D SMA Dist from 200D (%)
Key Metrics Explained:
  • 200D SMA: The ultimate baseline for long-term institutional health. We only screen for stocks above this line to ensure we aren't catching a "falling knife."
  • Dist from 200D (%): Shows how far the stock has pulled back toward its long-term baseline. A smaller positive number means it is closer to major institutional support.
  • Maximum RSI Threshold: A value of 30 or below is mathematically "oversold." Setting this threshold to 30 strictly isolates stocks experiencing extreme, irrational short-term fear.
