How to Read This Page

This page has two parts. The cards at the top tell you how "hot" your portfolio is as a whole — think of it like a fuel gauge for risk. The table below tells you, for each individual holding, how much that one holding is contributing to the overall reading. A ticker doesn't turn red because it's a "bad" stock — it turns red because of how it's currently behaving in the context of everything else you own. The three reasons below cover everything that can push a ticker toward red.

1. It's carrying a big share of your risk

Every holding contributes a slice to your portfolio's total day-to-day "how much could I lose" number. A large position, or a position that swings up and down a lot in price, naturally eats up a bigger slice of that risk budget than a small, calm holding does.

Shown in the table as Marginal VaR Contribution — the higher the percentage, the more of your portfolio's overall risk this one ticker alone is responsible for.

2. It moves in lockstep with things you already own

The whole point of holding several different things is that when one goes down, another might hold steady or go up, smoothing out the ride. But if two of your holdings tend to rise and fall together — say, two companies in the same industry reacting to the same news — then owning both doesn't actually spread your risk out. You've effectively made one bigger bet, not two smaller ones.

Shown in the table as Max Correlation — how closely this ticker's price movements match your most similar other holding. Close to 1.0 means "moves almost identically"; close to 0 means "moves independently."

3. It's getting close to its stop-loss level

Behind the scenes, every holding has a calculated "if it drops to here, the setup is broken" price level, based on how much that stock normally wobbles day to day. The closer today's price has drifted toward that level, the less room is left before a small further dip would mean a meaningfully worse outcome — even if nothing else about the company has changed.

Shown in the table as Stop Distance — the percentage gap between the current price and that calculated level. A small percentage means the price is right up against it.

TierWhat it means in plain terms
GreenThis holding (or your whole portfolio) is behaving normally on all three fronts — no single thing stands out as a risk concentration right now.
YellowAt least one of the three things above is elevated. Not an emergency, but worth a glance before adding more to this position or a similar one.
RedOne or more of the three things above is stretched. This is the holding (or the portfolio-wide combination of holdings) doing the most to drive your overall risk reading right now.

A note on the top-of-page cards: when the "All Accounts" card is Red, you'll see a small "Risk Paused" badge appear next to BUY-style signals elsewhere in the app (Portfolio, Watchlist, Stock Detail). That's a visual heads-up only — nothing is blocked or executed automatically, since this app doesn't place trades for you.
