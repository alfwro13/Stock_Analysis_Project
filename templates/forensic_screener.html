How to Read This Table

The Forensic Screener applies three independent models from academic accounting research to your holdings' annual financial statements. Each model asks a different question: Is this company getting fundamentally stronger or weaker? (Piotroski), Is it at risk of insolvency? (Altman), and Are its reported earnings trustworthy? (Beneish). These are lagging signals — they reflect the past year's filings, not today's price — which makes them ideal for catching slow-moving structural deterioration that doesn't show up in a price chart until it's too late.

Piotroski F-Score (0–9)

Developed by Stanford professor Joseph Piotroski in 2000, the F-Score is a 9-point checklist that awards 1 point for each criterion met and 0 for each failed. It tests three dimensions of financial health using the most recent annual report vs. the prior year:

  • Profitability (4 points): Positive return on assets, positive operating cash flow, improving ROA year-on-year, cash earnings exceeding accounting earnings (quality of profit).
  • Leverage & Liquidity (3 points): Long-term debt ratio declining, current ratio improving, no new shares issued (dilution test).
  • Operating Efficiency (2 points): Gross margin improving, asset turnover (revenue / assets) improving.

Altman Z-Score

Created by NYU professor Edward Altman in 1968 and refined for non-manufacturers, the Z-Score predicts the probability of corporate bankruptcy within two years. The Z' (prime) formula used here applies to service companies, retailers, and financials:

Z' = 6.56·(Working Capital / Assets)
   + 3.26·(Retained Earnings / Assets)
   + 6.72·(EBIT / Assets)
   + 1.05·(Book Equity / Total Liabilities)

The alert threshold is < 1.81 (entry into the lower half of the grey zone). N/A is common for UK and German-listed stocks where book equity data is not returned by yfinance.

Beneish M-Score

Developed by Messod Beneish in 1999, the M-Score is an 8-variable statistical model that detects whether a company's reported earnings are likely to have been manipulated. It compares two consecutive annual filings and looks for patterns that preceded known accounting frauds:

  • DSRI — Days Sales Receivables Index: receivables growing faster than revenue signals inflated revenue.
  • GMI — Gross Margin Index: declining margins can incentivise manipulation.
  • AQI — Asset Quality Index: rising intangibles or deferred costs vs. assets.
  • SGI — Sales Growth Index: high-growth firms face more pressure to maintain growth.
  • DEPI — Depreciation Index: slowing depreciation rate inflates reported income.
  • SGAI — SG&A expense Index: rising overheads relative to revenue.
  • LVGI — Leverage Index: increasing debt pressure.
  • TATA — Total Accruals to Total Assets: high accruals relative to cash earnings.

The Flags Column

A ticker shows a flag when it breaches at least one distress threshold. Multiple flags on the same holding are a stronger signal — a company with a low F-Score and a high M-Score is both structurally weakening and potentially misrepresenting its finances.

F<4 — Piotroski below 4: fundamental decay across multiple criteria. Z<1.81 — Altman in or below the grey zone: elevated insolvency probability. M>-1.78 — Beneish above the manipulation threshold: statistical earnings quality concern. ✓ Clean — No thresholds breached on the current annual data.
Important Limitations
  • Annual data only. These models use annual financial statements. A company can deteriorate significantly between filings without the scores updating.
  • Lagging signal. The models reflect what has already happened, not what will happen. A low F-Score or high M-Score does not guarantee further decline, and a high F-Score does not guarantee safety.
  • ETFs and funds always return N/A — they publish portfolio holdings, not income statements or balance sheets in the standard form these models require.
  • UK and European stocks (e.g. .L, .DE) often return N/A for Altman Z — Yahoo Finance does not reliably return book equity data for non-US tickers, making the Z' formula impossible to compute.
  • Not a recommendation. These are screening signals. A manipulation flag means "investigate the annual report", not "sell immediately". Always verify against the actual filings.
  • Update frequency. Data is refreshed monthly on the 1st. Scores only update when a new annual filing period is available from Yahoo Finance.
