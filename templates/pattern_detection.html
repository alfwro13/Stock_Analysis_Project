All Covered Patterns

Every pattern this scan can detect, grouped by the direction it signals once confirmed.

Bullish

Inverse Head & Shoulders — three swing lows where the middle (head) is lower than the two outer shoulders. Confirms on a close above the neckline drawn through the two intervening swing highs.

Double Bottom — two troughs of near-equal depth separated by one qualifying peak. Confirms on a close above the peak (resistance) level.

Bull Flag — a sharp rally (the pole) followed by a brief, mildly downward-sloped parallel channel. Confirms on a close above the upper channel line.

Bull Pennant — the same pole prerequisite as a Bull Flag, but the pause is a short converging micro-triangle rather than a parallel channel. Confirms on a close above the falling resistance line.

Ascending Triangle — a flat resistance level with a rising support line. Confirms on a close above resistance.

Symmetrical Triangle (Bullish) — a falling resistance line and rising support line converging together, direction unknown until it breaks. Confirms on a close above the falling resistance line.

Falling Wedge — both resistance and support slope downward, but resistance falls faster, forcing convergence — selling pressure is exhausting even as price still slides. Confirms on a close above the falling resistance line.

Volatility Squeeze (Bullish) — Bollinger Bands contracting fully inside the Keltner Channel, then a decisive close above the upper Bollinger Band once the squeeze releases.

NR4 / NR7 Narrow Range (Bullish) — the smallest True Range of the trailing 4 or 7 bars, followed by a decisive close above that bar's own high.

Parabolic Stretch (Oversold) — price closes unusually far below its 200-day SMA (by Z-score), statistically due for a bounce back toward the mean.

Bullish Divergence — price makes a new low while RSI makes a higher low, a leading sign selling pressure is fading. Confirms on a close through the level between the two extremes.

Bullish Engulfing / Hammer — a 1-2 bar rejection candle only counted near a price extreme (below the lower Bollinger Band or RSI oversold), used to confirm a larger bullish setup.

Bearish

Head & Shoulders — three swing highs where the middle (head) is taller than the two outer shoulders. Confirms on a close below the neckline drawn through the two intervening swing lows.

Double Top — two peaks of near-equal height separated by one qualifying trough. Confirms on a close below the trough (support) level.

Bear Flag — a sharp decline (the pole) followed by a brief, mildly upward-sloped parallel channel. Confirms on a close below the lower channel line.

Bear Pennant — the same pole prerequisite as a Bear Flag, but the pause is a short converging micro-triangle rather than a parallel channel. Confirms on a close below the rising support line.

Descending Triangle — a flat support level with a falling resistance line. Confirms on a close below support.

Symmetrical Triangle (Bearish) — a falling resistance line and rising support line converging together, direction unknown until it breaks. Confirms on a close below the rising support line.

Rising Wedge — both resistance and support slope upward, but support rises faster, forcing convergence — upward momentum is failing even as price still climbs. Confirms on a close below the rising support line.

Volatility Squeeze (Bearish) — Bollinger Bands contracting fully inside the Keltner Channel, then a decisive close below the lower Bollinger Band once the squeeze releases.

NR4 / NR7 Narrow Range (Bearish) — the smallest True Range of the trailing 4 or 7 bars, followed by a decisive close below that bar's own low.

Parabolic Stretch (Overbought) — price closes unusually far above its 200-day SMA (by Z-score), statistically due for a pullback back toward the mean.

Bearish Divergence — price makes a new high while RSI makes a lower high, a leading sign momentum is fading. Confirms on a close through the level between the two extremes.

Bearish Engulfing / Shooting Star — a 1-2 bar rejection candle only counted near a price extreme (above the upper Bollinger Band or RSI overbought), used to confirm a larger bearish setup.
