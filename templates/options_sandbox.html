識 Who is This Tool For?

This sandbox is heavily aimed at short-term, swing, and event-driven trading (such as trading around upcoming Earnings Reports or Fed announcements).

Because these binary events rely heavily on immediate volatility spikes, the Python backend of this tool is specifically designed to only fetch the nearest upcoming expiration dates. Near-term options are the most mathematically sensitive to sudden price changes (Gamma) and volatility crushes (Vega), making them the ideal instruments for catching short-to-medium-term momentum shifts.

答 Options 101: What is an Option?

At its core, a financial Option is a legally binding derivative contract. It gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset (like a stock or ETF) at a strictly agreed-upon price, on or before a specific date. Because you are buying a "right" rather than the underlying stock itself, options offer tremendous leverage and capital efficiency.

The Two Types of Options

  • The Call Option (Bullish): Gives you the right to buy the stock at a specific price. You buy a Call when you expect the stock price to surge upward.
  • The Put Option (Bearish): Gives you the right to sell the stock at a specific price. You buy a Put when you expect the stock price to crash (acting like an insurance policy).

The 4 Anatomy Parts of a Contract

Every option traded in this sandbox relies on four required variables:

  • 1. Underlying Asset: The specific stock the contract is tied to (e.g., AAPL, NVDA, SPY).
  • 2. Strike Price: The agreed-upon target price. If you buy a Call with a $150 Strike, you lock in the right to buy the stock at exactly $150, even if the real market price moons to $300.
  • 3. Expiration Date: Options are time-bound contracts. The expiration date is the exact day the contract ceases to exist. Because time is constantly running out, options suffer from "time decay" (Theta). If the stock doesn't reach your target before this ticking clock hits zero, the contract expires worthless.
  • 4. Premium: The actual cash price you pay to purchase the contract. This represents the maximum possible risk/loss for an options buyer. Note: In standard US equity markets, one contract always controls 100 shares. A $2.00 premium listed in the chain will actually cost $200 in real cash.

当 How to Use the Sandbox

  1. Load the Chain: Enter a valid stock or ETF ticker (e.g., AAPL, SPY) and click "Fetch Chain" to retrieve live market data and the nearest options expirations.
  2. Select Expiration Date: Use the dropdown menu to select the specific day your contract will expire.
  3. Build Your Strategy: Click the "+" button next to any Call or Put to add it to your Strategy Builder panel. You can toggle between going Long (Buy) or Short (Sell), and adjust your contract quantity.
  4. Analyze the Payoff Chart: Click "Plot Payoff".
    • The X-axis represents the Underlying Stock Price at Expiration.
    • The Y-axis represents your Net Profit/Loss (P&L) in dollars.
    • The Zero-line is your Breakeven point. Green areas indicate profit, while red areas indicate loss.

ｧ Common Options Strategies

Understanding multi-leg options strategies is key to managing risk and taking advantage of different market conditions (directional moves, high volatility, or sideways chop). Here is a guide to standard setups you can build in the Sandbox.

Long Call / Long Put

The simplest directional bets. You are buying the right (but not the obligation) to buy or sell the stock at a specific strike price.

Bullish: Buy 1 Call
Bearish: Buy 1 Put
Use when you have high conviction in a large directional move. Risk is strictly limited to the premium paid, while reward is theoretically unlimited (for calls).

Straddle

A delta-neutral volatility strategy. You make money if the stock makes a massive, explosive move in either direction.

Buy 1 ATM Call
Buy 1 ATM Put
(Same Strike, Same Expiry)
Use right before major binary events (like Earnings Reports or Fed announcements) when you expect extreme volatility but are unsure of the direction.

Strangle

Similar to a Straddle, but uses Out-of-The-Money (OTM) options. It is much cheaper to construct but requires a substantially larger move to become profitable.

Buy 1 OTM Call
Buy 1 OTM Put
Use when you expect an even more violently explosive move than a straddle prices in, and want to risk less initial capital on the premium.

Vertical Spread (Debit)

A directional bet that artificially caps both your maximum loss AND your maximum profit. Cheaper than buying a naked call or put.

Bull Call Spread:
Buy 1 ATM Call
Sell 1 OTM Call (Higher Strike)
Use when you are moderately bullish/bearish but want to mitigate the effects of Theta (time decay) and IV crush by financing your long leg with a short leg.

Iron Condor

A non-directional, income-generating strategy. You are betting that the stock will remain flat and stay trapped between two outer boundaries.

Sell 1 OTM Put Spread
Sell 1 OTM Call Spread
(Creates a "Profit Tent")
Use when you expect low volatility and sideways market chop. You collect premium upfront and keep it if the stock expires between your short strikes.

Covered Call

An income-generating strategy where you sell someone else the right to buy your stock at a higher price.

Own 100 Shares of Stock
Sell 1 OTM Call
Use when you are neutral to slightly bullish on a stock you already own. You collect premium, which lowers your cost basis, but cap your upside if the stock moons.
