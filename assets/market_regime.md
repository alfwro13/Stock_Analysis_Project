## Market Regime

The **Market Regime Context** acts as the macro weather vane for the entire terminal. Instead of looking at individual stocks, it isolates systemic market risk.

### 1\. The Turbulence Index (Currently: `14.72`)

This is a proprietary mathematical indicator generated by your `regime_engine.py`. It doesn't look at whether the market is up or down; it measures **structural instability**.

The engine calculates this strictly through **Realized Volatility**: It takes the recent trading history of the benchmark index (S&P 500 for the US, FTSE 100 for the UK), calculates the daily continuous logarithmic returns, finds the Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) variance, and annualizes it. 

*(Note: The US index was historically blended with the VIX. It was shifted to pure EWMA Realized Volatility to guarantee mathematical parity between US and UK markets—ensuring a Turbulence Score of 25 represents the exact same level of mathematical chaos in London as it does in New York).*

A score of **14.72** is fundamentally low, meaning the market is behaving predictably and efficiently.

The engine calculates this by running two distinct data dimensions through an out-of-band thread:

- **Realized Volatility:** It takes the last 21 trading days of the S&P 500 (`SPY`), calculates the daily continuous logarithmic returns, finds the standard deviation, and annualizes it. This is the mathematical "reality" of what price action is doing on the charts.
    
- **Implied Volatility:** It pulls the current close price of the CBOE Volatility Index (`^VIX`), which represents Wall Street's forward-looking option hedging behavior.
    

The risk engine averages these two numbers together to create a smooth, highly responsive baseline metric. A score of **14.72** is fundamentally low, meaning the market is behaving predictably and efficiently.

### 2\. Current Classification: `Normal`

The system maps the Turbulence Index into three institutional buckets:

- **Normal (Index < 20):** Quiet, trend-following behavior. Correlates with a low VIX.
    
- **Volatile (Index 20 - 30):** Structural shifting, choppy ranges, or minor asset corrections.
    
- **Crash (Index ≥ 30):** High systemic distress, systemic liquidations, panic selling, or flash crashes.

### Systemic Macro Threat Levels (`macro_regimes`)

Yield acceleration maps into strict danger vectors, indicating valuation multiple contraction risks:

- 🔴 **RED THREAT:** \$V_{\\text{yield}} \\ge 3.5\\\\%\$ OR \$Y_{\\text{current}} \\ge 5.0\\\\%\$. Indicates immediate severe liquidation pressure across tech, growth, and high-leverage assets.
    
- 🟡 **YELLOW THREAT:** \$V_{\\text{yield}} \\ge 1.5\\\\%\$. Indicates interest rates are moving up with high momentum. Multiples are entering a compression phase.
    
- 🟢 **GREEN THREAT:** Gilt and Treasury parameters are stable or falling. Favorable macro conditions for standard stock selection and asset price expansion.

## 4. Downstream Screener Mutation Logic

The overnight screening orchestrator (`quant_screener.py`) actively consumes these macro inputs to rewrite its scanning parameters in real time.

### A. The "Flight to Safety" Circuit Breaker

When the database registers a `Volatile` or `Crash` volatility regime, the screener protects capital by imposing a long-term institutional trend constraint:

Python

```
# Contextual screening restriction during market turbulence
if regime_label in ['Crash', 'Volatile']:
    if sma_200 is not None and close_price > sma_200:
        results.append(row)
```

- **Normal Regime Strategy:** Allows aggressive breakout setups, momentum surges, and deep-value oversold reversals to populate freely.
    
- **Turbulent Regime Strategy:** Automatically scrubs and filters out any equity or ETF operating below its **200-day Simple Moving Average**, bypassing false bear-market rallies.
    

### B. Macro Trap Yield-Sensitive Vetoes

If the Systemic Macro Threat escalates to **RED** or **YELLOW**, the engine automatically scans for structural balance sheet vulnerabilities and cross-engine correlations:

Python

```
# Intermarket multiples compression filter
is_high_multiple = (pe is not None and pe > 30) or (debt_to_equity is not None and debt_to_equity > 1.5)
is_neg_corr = yield_correlation is not None and yield_correlation <= -0.3

if is_high_multiple and is_neg_corr:
    vetoed.append(row) # Automatically funnelled to the Vetoed List
```

Even if a stock flashes a technically perfect breakout pattern, the terminal flags it as a **"Yield Sensitive / Macro Trap"** and bars it from standard discovery feeds if it is highly indebted (\$D/E > 1.5\$), carries an elevated valuation multiple (\$P/E > 30\$), and falls whenever bond yields surge.
